# Six-Axis Design Critique Framework

Each project under critique is evaluated against six axes. Focus on the 2-4 axes with real signal — do not score every axis on every project.

## 1. Concept consistency

Does the stated intention show up in form, material, and detail, or only in the diagram?

Sub-criteria:
- Diagram-to-3D fidelity: does the building actually do what the diagram claims?
- Material logic: do material choices express the concept, fight it, or stay neutral?
- Detail discipline: does the joinery/expression match the conceptual rhetoric?

Tells of a weak project on this axis:
- Concept is named in plan but invisible in section.
- Heavy verbal scaffolding (multi-paragraph concept statement) holding up a generic massing.
- Materials chosen for "warmth" but applied as veneer over volumes that contradict warmth.

## 2. Scale & proportion

Right size for what it claims to be? Relationship to user, neighbors, program demands.

Sub-criteria:
- Human scale: doors, ceilings, thresholds in inhabitable proportions for the program.
- Urban/rural scale: silhouette and mass in appropriate dialogue with neighbors.
- Program scale: room sizes match function (a 130 sf "gallery" isn't).
- Internal proportional system: if a system is claimed (golden, modular), is it consistent or arbitrary?

Tells:
- A "monumental" gesture sized for a building twice the budget.
- Domestic spaces with civic ceiling heights or vice versa.

## 3. Circulation & program

Logic of movement and adjacency. Public/private, served/servant, formal/informal cleanly resolved?

Sub-criteria:
- Entry sequence: clear hierarchy from public arrival to inhabited core.
- Cross-program coordination: noise/quiet, wet/dry, secure/open boundaries respected.
- Vertical circulation: stairs/elevators sized + placed for actual flow, not symbol.
- Service routing: back-of-house has its own movement layer.

Tells:
- Visitors and staff use the same threshold.
- A processional axis that dead-ends.
- "Flexible" rooms with no acoustic/HVAC plan to actually be flexible.

## 4. Material & detail

Material choice supports the concept or fights it? Detail level consistent with intent?

Sub-criteria:
- Tactile coherence: surfaces the user touches reinforce the design intent.
- Joint logic: corners, transitions, edges resolved (or honestly expressed if not).
- Weathering / aging: how the building looks at year 1 vs year 20.
- Cost-honesty: high-budget detailing on the parts that get touched, lower elsewhere.

Tells:
- "Brutalist" project rendered with paper-thin reveals.
- Material palette of 11 species — incoherent.
- Fancy lobby, basement-grade BOH (when both are equally seen by users).

## 5. Context

Site, climate, culture. Building aware of where it is?

Sub-criteria:
- Site response: orientation, topography, sight lines exploited or fought.
- Climate response: shading, insulation, ventilation appropriate to local extremes.
- Cultural literacy: typology references that are legible to the local audience without being pastiche.
- Code/zoning awareness: the design lives within the actual envelope.

Tells:
- A glass facade due south in a hot-arid site without shading.
- A "village" form that's actually one massive bar building dressed up.
- Form decisions ignore the site's elevation change.

## 6. Innovation vs feasibility

How much of the ambition is actually buildable on the implied budget and schedule?

Sub-criteria:
- Structural feasibility: spans, cantilevers, transfers within reasonable means.
- Construction sequence: trades can actually access the work.
- Budget reality: signature moves don't consume the entire budget.
- Maintenance burden: who cleans the 40 ft glass wall in 5 years?

Tells:
- A 100 ft cantilever shown without acknowledgment of the back-end engineering.
- "Off-site prefab" claimed without a fab partner identified.
- Gold-plated envelope on a community-budget program.

## How to use the framework in critique

- Identify which 2-4 axes the project is making claims on. Critique those.
- Do not invent claims the designer didn't make. If a project doesn't claim sustainability, do not judge sustainability.
- Each issue raised cites which axis and what specific sub-criterion is failing.
- Each issue ends with a "what to investigate" prompt — never a closed verdict.
