# NYC Zoning Resolution Structure Reference

> **Era.** This file describes the **post-2024 NYC Zoning Resolution structure** with the **City of Yes amendments incorporated** (Housing Opportunity, Carbon Neutrality, Economic Opportunity). Pre-2024 section numbers are cross-referenced inline where renumbering occurred. The post-2024 structure is the default for new permit work; pre-2024 numbers remain useful for permits filed before the amendments took effect. **Verify every cited section against the live ZR PDF (zoningresolution.planning.nyc.gov) before quoting** — see `_provenance.md` for the verification protocol.

## Article-by-Article Overview

| Article | Coverage | Common queries land here |
|---|---|---|
| **I — General Provisions** | Title, district establishment, language construction, definitions | Definitions (§12-10), zoning map references, applicability and scope |
| **II — Residence District Regulations** | R1–R10 districts: use, bulk, yards, height, density, parking | FAR, lot coverage, height factor, sky exposure plane, dwelling unit factor (DUF), required yards (front/side/rear), accessory residential use, off-street parking for residences, Quality Housing Program |
| **III — Commercial District Regulations** | C1–C8 districts: use groups, bulk, yards, parking, signs | Use Groups 5–16, FAR for commercial, accessory parking, sign regulations, mixed-use buildings, commercial overlays on residential districts |
| **IV — Manufacturing District Regulations** | M1, M1-X, M2, M3 districts: use, bulk, performance standards | Use Groups 16–18, performance standards (noise, vibration, smoke, odor), FAR for manufacturing, M1 contextual variants |
| **V — Non-Conforming Uses and Non-Complying Buildings** | Pre-existing uses or buildings that do not conform to current zoning | Continued non-conforming use, change of non-conforming use, expansion limits, restoration after damage |
| **VI — Special Regulations Applicable to Certain Areas** | Waterfront, harbors, transit zones, mandatory inclusionary housing (MIH), Lower Density Growth Management Areas | Waterfront zoning, public access requirements, MIH-designated areas, transit-zone parking reductions |
| **VII — Administration** | Special permits, authorizations, certifications, BSA, City Planning Commission powers, modifications | §74-71 special permits, §73 authorizations, BSA variances and special permits, modifications, certifications |
| **VIII — Special Purpose Districts (Manhattan)** | Special Midtown District, Special Garment Center District, Special Lincoln Square District, etc. | District-specific use, bulk, height, plaza/setback bonuses for established Manhattan Special Districts |
| **IX — Special Purpose Districts (continued, Manhattan + selected)** | Special Hudson Yards District, Special West Chelsea District, Special Battery Park City District, etc. | Hudson Yards bulk and bonus, West Chelsea high line, mixed-use Special Districts |
| **X — Special Purpose Districts (Queens, Bronx, Brooklyn, Staten Island)** | Outer-borough Special Districts (Special Long Island City District, Special Downtown Brooklyn District, etc.) | Outer-borough Special District use, bulk, height, FAR bonuses |
| **XI — Special Purpose Districts (continued)** | Continued outer-borough and overlay Special Districts | Same as Article X — newer or smaller-area Special Districts |
| **XII — Special Purpose Districts (continued)** | Newer Special Districts | Recent additions (e.g., Special Coastal Risk District, Special Enhanced Commercial District) |
| **XIII — Special Purpose Districts (continued)** | Recently adopted Special Districts | Most recent additions |
| **XIV — Special Purpose Districts (continued)** | Most recently adopted Special Districts | Newest Special Districts |

## NYC Zoning Notation Conventions

Inside the Zoning Resolution, district names follow specific patterns:

| Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `R6` | Base residence district |
| `R6A`, `R6B`, `R6X` | Contextual variants of R6 (typically Quality Housing required, different envelope rules) |
| `C2-4` | Commercial overlay (intensity 4) on a residential district — the residential district determines bulk and yards; the C2 overlay determines what commercial uses are permitted on the ground floor |
| `M1-1`, `M1-2`, ..., `M1-6` | Manufacturing variants of M1 by FAR/parking intensity |
| `M1-X` (where X is a letter) | M1 contextual variants in specific neighborhoods |
| Special Districts | Always prefixed "Special ___ District" (e.g., "Special Hudson Yards District") |

Commercial overlays (C1-X, C2-X) are mapped on top of residential districts. The underlying residence district (e.g., R6) governs bulk; the overlay (e.g., C2-4) governs ground-floor commercial use permissibility.

## Decision Path for Identifying the Right Section

1. Is the question about a defined term? → §12-10 first.
2. Is the question about a procedure (variance, special permit, certification)? → Article VII.
3. Is the question about a specific Special District (named like "Special ___ District")? → Articles VIII–XIV, look up by name in `outline.md`.
4. Is the question about a base district (R, C, or M)?
   - Residence (R1–R10) → Article II.
   - Commercial (C1–C8) → Article III. (Commercial overlays follow Article III for use; underlying residence rules follow Article II for bulk.)
   - Manufacturing (M1–M3) → Article IV.
5. Is it about waterfront, MIH, or a special area type? → Article VI.
6. Is it about a non-conforming use or building? → Article V.

If the question involves multiple districts (e.g., "comparison of FAR in R6 vs R7"), read each district's relevant section separately and quote both with citations.
