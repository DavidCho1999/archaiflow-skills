# OCIP / CCIP / Traditional — Decision Criteria

Wrap-up insurance (OCIP = Owner-Controlled Insurance Program; CCIP = Contractor-Controlled Insurance Program) consolidates CGL, Workers' Comp, and Excess for the owner / GC / all enrolled subs into a single program. Decision is driven by project size, owner sophistication, sub mix, and state rules.

---

## What wrap-up does

A wrap-up is a single insurance program — typically purchased and administered by the owner (OCIP) or GC (CCIP) — that provides:

- Workers' Compensation for all enrolled on-site labor.
- General Liability for owner, GC, and all enrolled subs.
- Excess / Umbrella over the GL.
- Sometimes Builder's Risk (less common, more often separate).

Subs enroll in the wrap and **deduct the value of their existing CGL + WC + Excess premiums from their bid** (or refund post-bid). The wrap administrator handles claims, audits, and certificates.

---

## OCIP vs. CCIP

| | OCIP | CCIP |
|---|------|------|
| Controlled by | Owner | GC / CMAR |
| Premium savings to | Owner | Owner (via reduced sub bids) and / or GC |
| Common on | Public projects (state, federal, healthcare, K-12, transit) | Private projects (commercial, residential developer-driven) |
| Owner staffing required | High (or third-party administrator) | Low |
| State enforcement | Some states (CA, NY, FL, TX) regulate OCIP separately | Less regulated |

## Decision criteria

### Project size

| Construction value | Wrap-up makes sense? |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| < $10M | Generally no — administrative cost outweighs premium savings |
| $10M – $25M | Marginal — depends on sub-heavy mix and owner sophistication |
| $25M – $100M | Yes — typical wrap-up sweet spot |
| > $100M | Yes — wrap-up + tower of excess |

### Single-site vs. rolling

- **Single-site** wrap-ups are easier to administer and audit. Most wrap-ups are single-site.
- **Rolling** (multi-site, multi-year) wrap-ups require more sophisticated owner / administrator and are typically used by repeat-builder owners (developers, public agencies).

### Owner sophistication

- Owner needs in-house risk management OR a third-party wrap administrator (TPA).
- Federal / state / municipal owners often have wrap programs through their procurement offices.
- Private developers often hire wrap brokers + TPAs.

### Subcontractor mix

- **Small-sub-heavy projects** (many small subs each carrying their own CGL + WC) benefit most — wrap pulls duplicated coverage costs out of every sub bid, often producing 2–5% net savings on subcontractor packages.
- **Large-sub-heavy projects** (a few large mech / electrical / curtainwall subs with sophisticated insurance programs) save less — large subs often have better-rate insurance than the wrap pool.

### State regulation

State-by-state regulation; verify with the broker. Examples:

- **California** — Specific OCIP rules under Insurance Code §11751.82. Wrap-up must meet specific notice and disclosure rules; sub credit must be calculated per state methodology.
- **New York** — NY Workers' Comp Board enforces OCIP enrollment; sub WC certs still required for non-enrolled scope (off-site fabrication).
- **Florida** — State Construction Industry Licensing Board enforces wrap-up rules.
- **Texas** — More flexible, but still requires registration and specific notice provisions.

### Trade exposure

- High-exposure trades (steel erection, demo, scaffold, deep excavation, hot work) are the wrap's value driver — those trades have the highest WC + GL premiums, and consolidating them through one program with good loss-control yields the largest savings.
- Low-exposure trades (finishes, FF&E install) have low base premiums; consolidating them yields little.

## Recommendation patterns

### When to recommend OCIP

- Public sector or public-funded project > $25M.
- Owner with in-house risk manager or contracted TPA.
- Project with high-exposure trades (heavy structural, deep excavation, abatement).
- Sub-heavy procurement strategy (many small-to-mid subs).
- State that has a clear OCIP regulatory framework (CA, NY, FL).

### When to recommend CCIP

- Private commercial / residential developer with experienced GC / CMAR.
- GC has existing CCIP infrastructure (large GC firm with TPA).
- Single-site, single-phase project.

### When to recommend traditional (no wrap)

- Project < $10M.
- Multi-site, fast-track, rolling subs that defeat consolidation.
- Owner without risk management capacity AND no TPA budget.
- Subs are predominantly large with their own better-rate coverage.

---

## Common pitfalls

- **Off-site fabrication** is typically **not** covered by wrap (steel fabricator, modular pre-fab, curtainwall fab) — confirm CGL + WC certs from off-site fabricators.
- **Auto Liability** is typically **not** in wrap — each sub carries own auto.
- **Professional Liability** is **never** in wrap — architects / engineers / CMAR carry separately.
- **Sub credit calculation** — owner needs to confirm subs deducted wrap-equivalent premium from bid; common audit finding.
- **Closeout claims tail** — wrap-up programs typically run 2–10 yr post-completion to handle products / completed-operations claims; budget for tail premiums.
- **Workers' Comp dispute** — if a worker injury occurs and there's a question whether the worker was enrolled in wrap or covered by sub's own WC, claim disputes can drag.

---

## What to ask the broker

1. Has owner / GC done wrap-ups before? At what scale?
2. What is the state regulatory framework? (CA / NY / FL / TX have specific rules.)
3. What is the high-exposure trade exposure? (Demo, structural steel, deep excavation, abatement.)
4. What is the sub mix? (Small-many vs. large-few.)
5. What is the project schedule? (Phasing affects wrap administration.)
6. Who will administer the wrap? (In-house owner, TPA, broker?)
7. What is the closeout tail requirement?

The broker delivers a wrap feasibility study with these inputs and returns a recommendation with actual market pricing.
